# Content Sources & Attribution

Source-of-truth for the canonical content shipped in this repo. The classical
books were generated by `tools/clean_content.py` from `da-xue/content/books/`;
every canonical translation is also labeled per-unit in the JSON
(`canonical_translations[]` with `translator` / `year` / `license` /
`source_url`).

This file exists because the original provenance lived only in a tool
docstring (`da-xue/tools/import_line_translations.py:1-16`) — buried, not
deliberate. It is now first-class.

The library is organized by two facets — **tradition** and **form** — defined
in [`TAXONOMY.md`](./TAXONOMY.md). The sections below follow tradition.

### On the translator choice

The v1 canon ships **James Legge** as the seeded canonical translator. This is
the translation that was already aligned in the source project — chosen because
it is public domain and was available on Wikisource, not because it was
deliberately selected over alternatives. We name that honestly rather than
pretending it was a curated decision.

**What YellowBridge uses.** YellowBridge's side-by-side Lunyu is *also* Legge
(explicitly cited as "The Chinese Classics, Vol I: Confucian Analects, James
Legge, 1891"). So our seeded Lunyu matches what a reader sees on YellowBridge.

**Modern alternatives (Lau, Slingerland, Watson, Ames & Rosemont).** These are
copyrighted and are **not stored verbatim** in this repo. Users may link to
them, but we do not reproduce their text. If a modern translation is ever
licensed, it joins `canonical_translations[]` per line.

### Known limitation of Legge's Mengzi

Legge renders 利 as "profit" throughout, which actively distorts Mencius's
argument (the opening exchange with King Hui of Liang *turns on* rejecting
"profit" as a frame). D.C. Lau's "benefit" is widely considered more faithful.
This is the single weakest spot in the v1 seed; we name it rather than hide it.

### Shi Jing — v1 (partial English coverage)

The Shi Jing (诗经, Classic of Poetry) ships as v1 with **partial** English
coverage. We name the two gaps honestly rather than hide them.

**Two Legge translations exist, and we ship the lesser one.** Legge translated
the Shi Jing twice:

| Edition | Year | Coverage | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| *The She King* (Chinese Classics, Vol. IV) | 1871 | **Complete** — all 305 odes, Mao order | ctext.org (ToS forbids automated download) / Internet Archive OCR (mediocre) |
| *The Shih King* (Sacred Books of the East, Vol. III) | 1879 | **Abridgment** — "Religious Portions of the Shih King", ~100 excerpted odes/stanzas, books reordered | en.wikisource.org (proofread, API-permitted) |

We ship the **1879 abridgment** because it is the only license-clean,
ToS-permitted, *proofread* source. The complete 1871 edition would require
either scraping ctext (forbidden — will get you IP-banned) or cleaning IA OCR
of Legge's heavy Wade-Giles apostrophe diacritics. Neither is done. This is
the same honest-gap discipline the chengyu-catalog uses (472/1000 untranslated).

**Coverage is 94/259 odes (36%)**, biased by the abridgment's "religious
portions" framing: the 颂 (sacrificial odes) are 100% covered, 大雅 83%, but
国风 (the folk songs, including the famous 关雎 Guan Ju) only 12% — Legge
judged the love songs and laments as having less "religious" content.

**Chinese-side gap: 259 of 305 odes.** zh.wikisource's 诗经 TOC itself omits
~46 odes (mostly short 周颂 pieces and a few duplicates absorbed by neighboring
odes). The ingestion script (`tools/ingest_shi_jing.py`) walks the zh-ws TOC,
so these are absent from the Chinese side too — not just untranslated. Filling
this requires a different Chinese source (e.g. ctext, same ToS constraint).

**Alignment safeguard.** The SBE abridgment reorders the books (颂 first) and
excerpts individual stanzas, so positional mapping to Mao order would pair the
wrong English with the wrong Chinese ode. Each Legge ode page's `{{header}}`
carries the ode's traditional characters; we match on those (not position) so
an English translation only attaches to the ode it actually translates. Odes
without a confident title match ship untranslated rather than misaligned — a
wrong pairing is worse than no translation.

### Bai Jia Xing — v1 (Chinese only)

The Bai Jia Xing (Hundred Family Surnames) is ingested from
zh.wikisource.org/wiki/百家姓 (anonymous Song-dynasty primer, public domain)
via `tools/ingest_bai_jia_xing.py`. No English translation is shipped and
none is seeded: the text is a rhymed catalogue of surnames with no prose to
translate, so the reader surfaces pinyin and per-surname glosses (CC-CEDICT)
instead. This is the same honest-gap discipline the chengyu-catalog uses for
its untranslated idioms — named rather than hidden. The 三百千 primer triad
is now complete.

### Sanguo Yanyi — v1

The 1925 Brewitt-Taylor translation is public domain (US since 2021-01-01;
UK/EU since 2009, Brewitt-Taylor died 1938). The translation text is complete
and usable; the 1959 Tuttle reprint's separate copyright covers only its
added maps and Roy Andrew Miller's introduction, not the translation.

The previous SOURCES.md note — "poem English on prose units; ~8.6% of units
have no English" — described the state in the upstream `da-xue` ingest
output, not this repo. In `the-big-learn`, sanguo was a deferred book and
**0% of units served English** (the `generated_annotation.layers.translation_en`
scratch field was never promoted to `canonical_translations[]`). The two
numbers measured different stages of one broken pipeline.

**Current state (this repo, v1):**

| Layer | Coverage | Source | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prose (6,345 / 7,394 units, 85.8%) | Brewitt-Taylor 1925, semantically aligned | public domain |
| Verse — poems, opening, closers (1,049 / 7,394, 14.2%) | GLM-generated, **labeled `source: "llm"`** | machine-generated |

Brewitt-Taylor omits every Chinese verse insert, so the 1,049 poem units
have no PD human rendering. We fill them with GLM and **label them** —
every `source: "llm"` entry carries a byline naming the generator and
license (e.g. "GLM · 2026 · machine-generated"), so the reader can see
machine provenance on the line itself. This is an explicit, narrow
exception to the project's documented stance against seeding machine
translation (chengyu catalog provenance note; migration 005). It is named
here rather than hidden. Spot-audit across chapters 1, 25, 50, 75, 100,
120 confirmed the verse translations are faithful (the famous 临江仙
opener, the Red Cliffs poem, Guan Yu's integrity couplets, Hua Tuo the
physician, the closing finale) — real translations, not word salad.

**Prose alignment — semantic, not length-only.** The prose aligner owning
the data (`tools/sanguo_align_semantic.py`) asks GLM for a
paragraph→unit-index *mapping*; the English pasted into
`canonical_translations[]` stays verbatim Brewitt-Taylor and the model
only decides alignment. A `validate_mapping` guard tolerates the two
failure modes GLM exhibits on long chapters (overlap and small gaps) by
deterministically repairing them; units that lose all paragraphs to
repair fall back to length-DP individually, so every prose unit still gets
*some* English. `tools/validate_sanguo.py` confirms 0 prose gaps and 0 LLM
leaks across all 120 chapters.

**On the validator's class-B outliers.** `validate_sanguo.py` currently flags 143
prose units as length-ratio outliers >3σ — *more* than the length-DP
baseline (113). This is expected and correct: semantic alignment does not
preserve length ratios. When Brewitt-Taylor expands a terse classical unit
into a long English speech, or when a unit introduces an embedded poem
that Brewitt-Taylor renders in full, the ratio spikes even though the
alignment is right. Spot-audits confirmed this (ch.120's final unit at
ratio 6.1 correctly carries the 340-word closing poem that the Chinese
「古风一篇」 introduces). Class B is a "review these" list, not a defect
count.

Tools: `tools/sanguo_align.py` (length-DP baseline, original),
`tools/sanguo_align_semantic.py` (GLM prose, current owner),
`tools/generate_sanguo_poems.py` (GLM verse),
`tools/validate_sanguo.py` (audit). The three GLM-gated scripts read
`GLM_API_KEY` and support `--dry-run` for no-key verification.


### Chengyu Catalog

Not sourced from `da-xue`'s 3,000-idiom catalog (which had ~13% bad
translations from a Google-Translate fallback, and no pinyin). Rebuilt
separately from the curated 1,000-idiom set with pinyin and CC-CEDICT-only
translations. See `tools/build_chengyu.py` and the chengyu catalog's own
`catalog.json` provenance fields. Carried as an idiom reference (`form: "idiom"`); no tier
(it is a reference layer over the rest of the library, not a single text).

**Origin links.** Where an idiom is coined verbatim in a classical text we
ship, the unit carries an `origin` link to the source line — so a reader on
见贤思齐 jumps to Lunyu 4:22. Searched books are origin sources only (Lunyu,
Mengzi, Daodejing, Zhong Yong, Da Xue, Sunzi Bingfa); Sanguo Yanyi and the
primers are excluded because they *use* idioms but coin almost none, and
linking them would mislabel usage as origin. Coverage is partial (~50 of
1,000 idioms) and grows as more classical texts ship; unmatched idioms simply
carry no origin. Built by `tools/attach_chengyu_origins.py`.

## Character reference data

`references/characters/index.json` (8.9 MB) carries pinyin / zhuyin /
simplified / traditional / English senses per character, derived at render time
to populate the char-by-char breakdown table. Upstream: `makemeahanzi`,
`OpenCC`, Unicode Unihan, `cjkvi-ids`, with GLM-enriched antonyms/synonyms.

## Word-level gloss data

Each classical-book reading unit carries a `word_spans[]` field (built by
`tools/build_word_gloss.py`) that segments the line into multi-char compounds
with pinyin + gloss, so clicking a char surfaces the *token* it belongs to, not
just the char's own gloss. Sources are layered, longest-match-wins:

1. **Curated classical overrides** —
   `references/characters/word-gloss-overrides.json`. Hand-verified classical
   senses for compounds where CC-CEDICT is wrong (大学 = "university" not "The
   Great Learning") or absent (明明德, 止于至善). Defensible from James Legge's
   translations and published zhuyin editions. Tagged `source:
   "classical-override"` in the span.
2. **CC-CEDICT** — `https://cc-cedict.org/`, **CC BY-SA 3.0**. Modern-Mandarin
   compound glosses for everything the overrides don't cover. The reader's
   provenance label distinguishes these from the curated overrides so the user
   knows a gloss is modern-Mandarin (a starting point, not a classical
   authority). Tagged `source: "CC-CEDICT"`.
3. **Per-char fallback** — single-char span with no gloss, for any CJK rune
   the upper layers don't cover. The per-char breakdown (above) handles the
   gloss. Tagged `source: "char"`.

CC-CEDICT is downloaded once per build to `/tmp` (shared cache with
`tools/build_chengyu.py` via `tools/_cedict.py`). The chengyu catalog is
excluded — its units already carry their own canonical translations.
